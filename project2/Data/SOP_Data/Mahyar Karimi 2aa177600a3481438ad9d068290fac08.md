**Additional Advice**

(1) Focus on research. What research projects you like to join, and more importantly, *why* you want to join these projects. Try to have concrete examples and explanations. Make it clear that you know about the research problems, and that you are making an informed choice. 

(2) Avoid ambiguity as much as possible. If you think you are making a general statement with so much wiggle room, so will the people who will read your SoP. So, always try to be precise and clear about what you say in your SoP. 

(3) Customize your SoP for each university. Make it clear that you know about the professors that you mention in your SoP. One straightforward way is to mention a *recent* paper, or their current research.

(4) Keep it short enough. People who read your SoP have to read SoPs from many other applicants. So, keep your SoP engaging and easy to follow. A lengthy SoP is no good, and neither is an SoP that only you can read. 

(5) Do not be afraid to make it technical. Your SoP will be read by people who, more or less, share some background with you. So they can understand technicalities. Again, don't make it boring. Also, don't write things you are unsure about, exactly because readers of your SoP are informed people and can tell if you are wrong. You don't want them to think you are making wrong statements.

(6) Start writing your SoP a few months before your deadlines. You definitely need to majorly refine it at least once or twice. Be early so that you have enough time to make refinements. It's not a good idea to start writing a week before a deadline.
